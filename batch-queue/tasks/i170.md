The loop judges and files the findings of a thread's pauses when the thread reaches its final exit (GitHub issue #170)

A pause is the most expensive thing a run can do: it stops until someone comes. On 2026-11-25 five threads paused 14 times and the improvement loop decided nothing about any of the pauses. The segments that ended in those pauses recorded 66 finding rows; 40 distinct findings of them (thread, unit and cause) appear in no `loop_started` record's `fingerprints`, so no friction judge was asked about them. They are the causes of the pauses themselves: `gap_escalation`, `judge_blocked:<criterion>`, `no_change`, `retries_spent`, `check_fail:<unit>`, `escalation:tool_access`, `engine_error` and others.

The reason is in the hand-off. The ticket of a final exit holds the fingerprints of the last segment only: `improve/record.py` `record_exit` reads `unjudged_pause_findings` only for exit code 4 with reason `person` (SIL-B28, a stop at a pause). A thread that pauses, is resumed and ends with exit 0 never brings its pauses to a loop. An open loop issue whose fingerprint was recorded twice in paused segments that day got no "seen again" comment (SIL-B2). And the friction judge's input holds the record kinds of the last segment only (`improve/child.py` `prompt`), so a pause finding that reached it would come without what answered the pause.

Wanted: at every final exit of a thread the loop judges and files the findings of the thread's earlier segments that ended in a pause and that no loop has judged, each with what answered the pause. The area is the engine (`plugins/tempergraph`), specs `runs-without-a-final-exit` (SIL-B27, SIL-B28, SIL-AC19 to SIL-AC21) and `self-improvement` (SIL-B2, SIL-B9, SIL-B17). The engine names no workflow (ADR `the-engine-names-no-workflow`).

Acceptance criteria:

1. A spec behaviour (SIL-B28 or one beside it) covers every final exit: the findings of earlier segments of the thread that ended in a pause, and that no loop of the thread judged, are findings of that exit.
2. Each such finding is judged once: a later final exit of the same thread (after a rerun or a resume) does not hand it to a judge again (the rule of SIL-AC21).
3. The friction judge's input names, for each such finding: the pause's reason, the decision that answered it, the kind of caller that decided, whether feedback was given (never its text), whether the workspace changed between the pause and the resume, and the verdict of the unit's next gate.
4. A finding of a paused segment whose fingerprint or cause matches an open loop issue adds its "seen again" comment at the thread's final exit, and no new issue.
5. An engine fixture: a thread that pauses on `gap_escalation`, is resumed with `proceed` and ends with exit 0 gives a ticket that holds that fingerprint, and the friction judge is asked about it with the six facts of criterion 3.
6. An engine fixture: the same thread with an open issue that holds the fingerprint leaves one comment and no new issue.
7. A thread stopped at a pause by a person behaves as before the change (SIL-AC19 to SIL-AC21 still pass).
8. No feedback text and no file content reaches the judge's prompt or `loop.jsonl`.

Out of scope: when a judged finding files (#171; a finding judged below the floor is still only recorded); new cause codes for a trip back, the attempt cap or a usage limit (#172); the session review of the paused segments' sessions; a thread that never reaches a final exit (SIL-B29 to SIL-B31 stand).

Likely files: `plugins/tempergraph/engine/src/tempergraph/improve/record.py` (`record_exit`, `unjudged_pause_findings`), `improve/child.py` (`prompt`, `process`), `improve/filing.py`, `docs/specs/runs-without-a-final-exit.spec.md`, `docs/specs/self-improvement.spec.md`, `tests/test_stop_at_pause.py`, `tests/test_loop_child.py`, `tests/test_improve.py`.
