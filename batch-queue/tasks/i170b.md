The friction judge is told the right facts about a pause: finish the change after its review, first of two runs (GitHub issue #170)

Since merge `fba4993c` every final exit of a thread hands the loop the findings of the thread's earlier segments that ended in a pause and that no loop of the thread was handed (SIL-B28), and the friction judge is told for each what answered the pause: the reason, the decision, the kind of caller, whether feedback was given, whether the workspace changed and the verdict of the unit's next gate (SIL-B37); no text of a run reaches the judge or `loop.jsonl` (SIL-B38). The area is the engine (`plugins/tempergraph`): specs `runs-without-a-final-exit` (SIL-B28, SIL-B37, SIL-AC39 to SIL-AC46), `self-improvement` (SIL-B9, SIL-B38), `loop-issues` (ISS-B8 to ISS-B10), `caller-provenance` (PRV-B11, PRV-B14), `worktree-mode` (WTR-B22) and the guide `docs/guides/improving-workflows.guide.md`. The engine names no workflow (ADR `the-engine-names-no-workflow`), so every behaviour is stated for any package and tested with an engine fixture.

A first run built the hand-off; its plan and notes are `plugins/tempergraph/docs/plans/i170.plan.md` and `plugins/tempergraph/docs/tasks/i170.triage.md`, `i170.handoff.md` and `i170.report.md`; this run writes its own plan and notes and leaves those as the record. Its code met its criteria. An independent review (`batch-queue/tasks/i170.review.md`, with a replay script in its appendix) then replayed the engine's functions over one machine's real threads: seven final exits of threads that paused 1 to 4 times, every pause answered by an agent. The hand-off works (a ticket of 1 finding becomes one of 17), but of the facts rows about a third are six times "not recorded", six findings of one thread are shown another segment's pause, and "workspace changed" is said at 9 of 11 pauses because the run's own commits count as a change. The pauses' own causes are judged and stay below the count; what files is base-rate noise and `agent_decided:escalation`.

Two runs are in progress and are merged before this one starts: i172 (`batch-queue/tasks/i172.md`, the cause codes) and i171b (`batch-queue/tasks/i171b.md`, the pattern rule made safe). This run starts from the batch head that holds both and does not rebuild what their criteria cover:

- Left to i172: a tool-access pause's refusal class (its cause becomes `tool_access:<refusal class>`, so the facts need no tool field), a pause of an ask unit classed `designed`, and the codes for a trip back, the attempt cap, an undelivered last stage and a usage limit.
- Left to i171b: filing by the count on one unstable "true" (the majority of recorded answers, its criterion 1, which already stops `agent_decided:escalation` filing on one answer), the brief's sentence on filing by the count, the bound on "seen again" comments, the reasons of a finding that does not file, a request counted once per thread, the machine-wide hold and `resume --dry-run`.

A second run follows this one (`batch-queue/tasks/i170c.md`, the operator's surface): the filing outbox, a ticket that only recorded using the pause findings up (review finding 9), and a reason an operator gives with a decision. This run leaves those alone.

The findings below are the work of this run (numbers are the review's):

1. A finding whose unit did not pause gets another segment's pause or an empty row: `improve/pause_facts.py` (`_facts`) takes the last pause of the finding's unit anywhere in the thread.
2. "Workspace changed" is read from the next `workspace` record, whose `external_change` compares the head with a `last_head` (`workspace.py`, `attach_workspace`) that is not refreshed when the run's own sessions or commands commit.
3. "Next gate" matches the unit and the child and not the item: after a pause at one item of a map the judge is told the first gate of the next item.
4. A decision with a ruling and no feedback reads as "feedback not given"; the decision row's touch class (`rescue`, `steer`, `designed`) is not a fact.
5. The judge's brief (`improve/child.py`, `BRIEF`) does not mention the section, and "The segment's records" covers the last segment only.
6. An issue filed for a pause finding is built from the last segment (`improve/issue_context.py`, `segment`): exit code 0, a run log without the finding's unit, no word of the pause.
7. `agent_decided:<kind>` (`improve/causes.py`) is a workflow fault for every pause an agent answers, also when that agent is the caller that started and supervises the run.
8. Exits 4 other than a person's stop, and exit 5, have no test with earlier pauses, and the guide's list of final exits is short.

Acceptance criteria:

1. Each finding of a segment that ended in a pause is told the pause that ended its own segment, whatever the finding's unit, and its row names the paused unit and item. A finding for which no pause, no decision and no gate is recorded gets no row. SIL-B37 states it. A fixture thread that pauses at unit A, is resumed, and pauses at unit B with a finding of unit A in that second segment gives a row for that finding with the reason and the unit of the pause at B; a thread of one pause gives no row of six "not recorded".
2. "Workspace changed" compares the head and the dirty state the engine records at the pause with those at the resume; commits and changes made by the run's own sessions and commands before the pause are not a change. A fixture whose stand-in step commits in the worktree, then pauses and is resumed with nothing done in between, gives "workspace unchanged"; the same with a commit made between the pause and the resume gives "workspace changed"; a run in place still gives "not recorded". `worktree-mode` states what the `workspace` record of a resume compares.
3. "Next gate" is the next gate of the paused unit, child and item. A fixture map that pauses at one item, is resumed with `proceed`, and whose next item's first gate is RETRY gives "next gate verdict `none`" for the pause's finding.
4. The facts say whether a ruling was given (never its text) and the touch class of the decision. A fixture decision with a ruling and no feedback gives "ruling given" and "feedback not given"; a canary word in the ruling is in neither the prompt nor `loop.jsonl` (SIL-B38's test gains it).
5. The judge's brief says what the section on pauses is and how an answer bears on attribution (at least: a `retry` that passes with the workspace unchanged points at the judge or the check and not at the step; a `proceed` with a ruling says a person or the supervising caller overrode the gate). A test asserts the sentences are in the prompt the judge session receives when a pause finding is in it, and that the i171b sentence on the count is still there.
6. The prompt shows, for each paused segment whose findings are in the ticket, the counts of the record kinds of its units and the ids of the criteria that blocked them, as it does for the last segment, each section headed by the segment's exit code and pause reason. A fixture of two paused segments and a final one gives three sections, and no text of a record is in them.
7. An issue or a "seen again" comment for a finding of a paused segment is built from that segment: its `## Run` names the segment's start, exit code 3 and pause reason, its `## Run log` holds that segment's records of the finding's unit, and it carries the facts line of criterion 1. `loop-issues` states it. A test reads the body the memory code host received.
8. A decision whose recorded caller is the caller recorded at the run's start (the same host and host session, PRV) yields `agent_decided:<kind>` classed `designed`: recorded, never judged and never filed. A decision by another agent still yields the fault. `self-improvement` states the class, and a fixture proves both cases.
9. At a final exit with code 4 and reason `stopped_by_request`, one with reason `attempt_cap`, and one with code 5, a fixture thread with an earlier pause gives a ticket that holds the pause's fingerprint under `pause_fingerprints` (one parametrised test), and the guide's paragraph on final exits lists every exit that SIL-B9 makes final.
10. The specs changed by this run state what the code does without naming any workflow, stay within the spec budget, and each new test carries the id of the spec criterion it proves; SIL-AC19 to SIL-AC21 and SIL-AC39 to SIL-AC46 still pass, changed only where a criterion above changes the fact they assert.

Out of scope, each to be filed as a follow-up issue and named in the report (the second run's subjects above are not filed again):

- `pause_fingerprints` do not reach the loop's proposer: at exit 0 the pause findings are judged and filed and never proposed for, at a person's stop they are (review finding 10).
- One finding per fingerprint in a ticket: a unit that paused several times shows the facts of its last pause, and a fingerprint that comes back in the final segment loses its facts row (review finding 11).
- The second scenario of SIL-B37 has no single test, and `drain_with_issue` clears an environment variable the suite's isolation already clears (review finding 12).
- A stop by request is a final exit that judges and files though the thread goes on; it has no cause code.
- A `blocked` pause caused by a capability the session lacks is class `designed` and never judged; the id of a failing check is not in `check_fail:<unit>`, and a finding's side cannot move from the workflow to the engine; a judge verdict that flips with the workspace unchanged has no cause code.
