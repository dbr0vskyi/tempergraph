Run log records the settings layer each timeout came from (GitHub issue #22)

BUD-B10 of the engine's spec `budgets-and-failures` says how a timeout is resolved: over the layers of spec `run-settings-and-overrides` (RST-B22: `flag`, `profile`, `project:overrides`, `user:overrides`, a switch arm, `workflow:unit`, `workflow:default`, `project:defaults`, `user:defaults`, `engine`), a judge never taking a step's value, and with the number and its source layer on every step and judge record. BUD-AC4 is the criterion for the record, and OBS-B7 of spec `observability-and-cost` lists "timeout and source" among the fields of `attempt_start`. Today the `attempt_start` record of the run log carries `timeout_s`, and its `sources` field names a layer only for a value that a switch arm set, so for most sessions the log does not say where the timeout came from. The code report of release 3 track R3-T3.1 (`plugins/tempergraph/docs/tasks/r3-t3-1.report.md`) left this open for the owner. The area is the engine (`plugins/tempergraph`). The engine names no workflow (ADR `the-engine-names-no-workflow`).

Acceptance criteria:

1. Every `attempt_start` record of a step session and of a judge session carries `timeout_source`, the name of the settings layer its timeout came from, beside `timeout_s` (BUD-AC4).
2. A judge with no timeout of its own, at the gate of a step that declares `timeout_s` 1800, records 600 with `timeout_source` `engine` (BUD-B10).
3. For the settings of the scenario of RST-B22 (user `defaults` with `steps.timeout_s` 900, project `overrides` with `code.timeout_s` 7200, `code` declaring 3000, `review` 1800, and `--set plan.timeout_s=1200`), the records carry `flag` for `plan`, `project:overrides` for `code`, `workflow:unit` for `review`, `user:defaults` for `triage` and `engine` for the judge of `code`.
