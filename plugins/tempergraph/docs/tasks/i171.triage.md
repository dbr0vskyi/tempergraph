## Acceptance criteria

1. `self-improvement` SIL-B18 states a second way for a friction finding to file: its fingerprint is recorded as a fault in at least `pattern_rule.min_runs` runs with at least `pattern_rule.min_distinct_requests` distinct requests inside the window, counted as SIL-B3 counts. The confidence floor then applies only to a finding below that count.
2. A finding that meets the count and that the friction judge marked `attributed: false` with a confidence at or above the floor does not file.
3. The spec says whether a finding that meets the count files when the friction judge's session gave no answer for it, and a test proves what it says.
4. The issue of a finding filed by the count carries the runs and requests counts, as an outcome finding's issue does.
5. `loop.jsonl` records, for each finding the friction judge answered, its fingerprint, `confidence` and `attributed`, and no text of the judge.
6. The daily cap (SIL-B20), the de-duplication by cause (SIL-B2), dry run (SIL-B6) and the side rules hold for a finding filed by the count as for any other.
7. An engine fixture: three runs with three requests and one fingerprint, each judged at 0.5, file one issue; two runs file none; a fourth run adds a "seen again" comment and no second issue.
8. The open question on the 0.75 floor in `self-improvement` is answered, or restated with the distribution measured on 2026-11-25 (64 answers, highest 0.70, median 0.50, 16 `attributed: false`).
9. The open owner question is kept in the spec: should a `review_repeat` cluster seen in several runs reach a person in any way but `improve backlog` and the loop summary? Until answered a finding made of review observations never files (SRV-B28).
