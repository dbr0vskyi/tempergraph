# Triage: i170b

## Task

`batch-queue/tasks/i170b.md` (GitHub issue #170, first of two runs): the friction judge is told wrong or empty facts about a pause. The task fixes eight findings of an independent review of the first run's hand-off (the segment's own pause, the workspace comparison, the item of the next gate, the ruling and the touch class, the brief, the per-segment sections, the issue built from the paused segment, a supervising caller's decision as `designed`, final exits 4 and 5 with earlier pauses). It starts from the batch head that holds i172 and i171b and rebuilds nothing of theirs. The area is the engine.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Each finding of a segment that ended in a pause is told the pause that ended its own segment, whatever the finding's unit, and its row names the paused unit and item. A finding for which no pause, no decision and no gate is recorded gets no row. SIL-B37 states it. A fixture thread that pauses at unit A, is resumed, and pauses at unit B with a finding of unit A in that second segment gives a row for that finding with the reason and the unit of the pause at B; a thread of one pause gives no row of six "not recorded".
2. "Workspace changed" compares the head and the dirty state the engine records at the pause with those at the resume; commits and changes made by the run's own sessions and commands before the pause are not a change. A fixture whose stand-in step commits in the worktree, then pauses and is resumed with nothing done in between, gives "workspace unchanged"; the same with a commit made between the pause and the resume gives "workspace changed"; a run in place still gives "not recorded". `worktree-mode` states what the `workspace` record of a resume compares.
3. "Next gate" is the next gate of the paused unit, child and item. A fixture map that pauses at one item, is resumed with `proceed`, and whose next item's first gate is RETRY gives "next gate verdict `none`" for the pause's finding.
4. The facts say whether a ruling was given (never its text) and the touch class of the decision. A fixture decision with a ruling and no feedback gives "ruling given" and "feedback not given"; a canary word in the ruling is in neither the prompt nor `loop.jsonl` (SIL-B38's test gains it).
5. The judge's brief says what the section on pauses is and how an answer bears on attribution (at least: a `retry` that passes with the workspace unchanged points at the judge or the check and not at the step; a `proceed` with a ruling says a person or the supervising caller overrode the gate). A test asserts the sentences are in the prompt the judge session receives when a pause finding is in it, and that the i171b sentence on the count is still there.
6. The prompt shows, for each paused segment whose findings are in the ticket, the counts of the record kinds of its units and the ids of the criteria that blocked them, as it does for the last segment, each section headed by the segment's exit code and pause reason. A fixture of two paused segments and a final one gives three sections, and no text of a record is in them.
7. An issue or a "seen again" comment for a finding of a paused segment is built from that segment: its `## Run` names the segment's start, exit code 3 and pause reason, its `## Run log` holds that segment's records of the finding's unit, and it carries the facts line of criterion 1. `loop-issues` states it. A test reads the body the memory code host received.
8. A decision whose recorded caller is the caller recorded at the run's start (the same host and host session, PRV) yields `agent_decided:<kind>` classed `designed`: recorded, never judged and never filed. A decision by another agent still yields the fault. `self-improvement` states the class, and a fixture proves both cases.
9. At a final exit with code 4 and reason `stopped_by_request`, one with reason `attempt_cap`, and one with code 5, a fixture thread with an earlier pause gives a ticket that holds the pause's fingerprint under `pause_fingerprints` (one parametrised test), and the guide's paragraph on final exits lists every exit that SIL-B9 makes final.
10. The specs changed by this run state what the code does without naming any workflow, stay within the spec budget, and each new test carries the id of the spec criterion it proves; SIL-AC19 to SIL-AC21 and SIL-AC39 to SIL-AC46 still pass, changed only where a criterion above changes the fact they assert.
